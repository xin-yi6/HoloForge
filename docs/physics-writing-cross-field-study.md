# Physics writing across communities

Historical nine-paper extension. See the [expanded study](physics-writing-expanded-study.md)
for the current 64-paper collection; this record retains its original scope.

This editorial companion extends the [sixteen-paper study](physics-writing-reading-study.md)
and [manuscript guide](physics-manuscript-writing.md). Nine additional papers
were inspected on 14 September 2026. The combined corpus has twenty-five
papers; the count is a record, not a quality target. These are original
editorial interpretations, not quotations, scientific audits or endorsements
by the authors. No private research example is needed to apply the lessons.

## Selection and reading depth

The additions span particle theory, condensed matter, nonequilibrium
statistical mechanics, quantum foundations and cosmology. An observational
paper provides a deliberate contrast to theory. The sample favors influential
historical work, so it has selection and survivorship bias; it is not a survey
of current submission standards or all physics communities. Original papers
are used as primary sources, not summaries about famous physicists.

Entries 1–3 and 5–7 were read through their main text. Entries 4, 8 and 9
were read in the sections specified below. Main-text reading includes the
abstract when present and the ending, but does not imply rederiving equations,
auditing references or reviewing later corrections. For scanned journal pages,
the reading concerns the named article, not adjacent articles on shared pages.
Versions identify the inspected text, not a scientific source-version clearance.

## Additional reading record

### 1. Weinberg: motivate a model through an obstacle

*A Model of Leptons*, published PRL 19, 1264–1266 (1967):
[publisher text](https://journals.aps.org/prl/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevLett.19.1264).
Read: main text, including the closing discussion of renormalizability.

The opening makes a proposed unification face concrete obstacles before
introducing its model. Masses, interactions and a neutral-current consequence
then form a connected argument; a remaining theoretical question is stated
explicitly at the end. Transfer: introduce machinery as an answer to a physical
obstacle. Do not infer that an unresolved question is harmless simply because
it also appeared in an influential historical paper.

### 2. Higgs: let a minimal example expose the mechanism

*Broken Symmetries and the Masses of Gauge Bosons*, published PRL 13,
508–509 (1964):
[publisher text](https://journals.aps.org/prl/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevLett.13.508).
Read: main text and the qualification concerning classical equations.

A compact model makes the central mechanism visible through the linearized
equations and the resulting degrees of freedom, before broader applicability
is discussed. Transfer: choose the simplest example that retains the essential
physics, then explain the generalization. The example's conceptual reach, not
its short length, is the relevant lesson; few equations alone do not constitute
a substantial result.

### 3. Haldane: isolate the assumption that matters

*Model for a Quantum Hall Effect without Landau Levels: Condensed-Matter
Realization of the "Parity Anomaly"*, published PRL 61, 2015–2018 (1988):
[publisher text](https://journals.aps.org/prl/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevLett.61.2015).
Read: main text and figure captions.

The model separates ingredients often encountered together. Distinct symmetry
changes are compared through their response, and the low-energy description
explains the phase structure. Transfer: make a comparison isolate a physical
assumption rather than just produce different curves. A deliberately idealized
model can be informative when the argument explains what its construction
establishes and what direct physical realization would still require.

### 4. Kitaev: build intuition before the general criterion

*Unpaired Majorana fermions in quantum wires*:
[cond-mat/0010440v2](https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0010440v2).
Read: abstract, introduction, section 1, the opening and final criterion of
section 2, and section 3. The intervening general proof was not fully reviewed.

Two solvable limits give the reader a physical picture before general
parameters and a bulk criterion are introduced. Possible realization is
discussed separately from the model construction. Transfer: use a limit to
teach the mechanism and explain which new question demands general machinery.
Keep a proposed realization distinguishable from a demonstrated one; do not
import historical statements about robustness without their conditions.

### 5. Jarzynski: state what an equation lets one learn

*A nonequilibrium equality for free energy differences*:
[cond-mat/9610209v1](https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/9610209v1).
Read: main text, including limiting cases, proof, applicability and simulations.

The new relation is contrasted with a familiar inequality, and its information
content is explained before the proof. Known limits orient the reader; the
ending distinguishes formal validity from practical sampling difficulty.
Transfer: explain what becomes calculable, then distinguish validity,
numerical accessibility and physical usefulness. An exact relation does not
automatically make its estimator inexpensive or reliable in practice.

### 6. Crooks: give assumptions and numerical illustrations different jobs

*Entropy production fluctuation theorem and the nonequilibrium work relation
for free energy differences*:
[cond-mat/9901352v4](https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/9901352v4).
Read: abstract and sections I–V, including figure captions.

The introduction connects two results through a concrete forward/reverse
process. The derivation specifies its assumptions; applicable classes and
long-time approximations then receive separate treatment. Numerical examples
clarify the distinction between an exact statement and an approximation.
Transfer: tell the reader whether a plot illustrates, tests or estimates a
result. A successful numerical illustration is not the proof of a theorem.

### 7. Bell: turn a conceptual disagreement into a precise test

*On the Einstein Podolsky Rosen paradox*, published Physics Physique Fizika
1, 195–200 (1964):
[publisher text](https://journals.aps.org/ppf/pdf/10.1103/PhysicsPhysiqueFizika.1.195).
Read: sections I–VI.

The paper translates a conceptual question into explicit assumptions, uses
illustrations to separate possible claims, and then derives a contradiction.
Its conclusion returns to an experimental distinction. Transfer: define what
two descriptions would predict differently before deploying formalism.
This is a lesson about exposing assumptions, not a substitute for modern
interpretive or experimental analysis of the theorem.

### 8. Guth: distinguish a mechanism from an unfinished scenario

*Inflationary universe: A possible solution to the horizon and flatness
problems*, published PRD 23, 347–356 (1981):
[publisher text](https://journals.aps.org/prd/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevD.23.347).
Read: abstract, section I, section III through the exponential-expansion
argument, the closing inhomogeneity argument of section V, and section VI.
The remaining derivations and appendix were not fully read.

The puzzles are made quantitative, a mechanism is proposed, and a serious
obstacle to the scenario is not hidden from the abstract or conclusion.
Transfer: separate the question a mechanism answers from the conditions still
needed for a viable overall model. This is a counterexample to requiring every
extension to be complete, not permission to leave the central asserted result
unsupported or to equate every incomplete project with a conceptual breakthrough.

### 9. Abbott and collaborators: connect evidence to an observation

*Observation of Gravitational Waves from a Binary Black Hole Merger*:
[1602.03837v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.03837v1), displaying the published
PRL 116, 061102 (2016) text.
Read: abstract, section I, opening of II and figure 1 caption, figure 4 caption,
section V.B, opening of VI through table I, and sections VII–VIII.

The result is stated early, while the exposition distinguishes the observed
signal, its physical interpretation, detection significance and parameter
uncertainty. Figure captions explain how data and reconstructions are displayed.
Transfer: make different evidential roles visible rather than collapsing them
into one confidence label. This observational architecture is a contrast case,
not a requirement that a theoretical model paper resemble a detection paper.

## Comparative lessons

These comparisons are editorial inferences from the records above:

- **Shared logic is not identical prose.** A minimal mechanism (2), a
  theorem and its conditions (6, 7), a model construction (3, 4), a proposal
  with a consequential obstacle (8), and an observation (9) require different
  evidence, even when all organize around a physical question.
- **An organizing equation needs an interpretation.** Explain what quantity,
  distinction or possibility it establishes (1, 3, 5). Do not leave the reader
  to infer that consequence from algebra alone.
- **A controlled example can carry a substantial argument.** Its job may be
  to expose a mechanism or distinguish assumptions (2–4, 7). Do not confuse
  computational volume with scientific contribution or use historical letters
  as a shortcut to judging a new manuscript's sufficiency.
- **Scope should sharpen the result.** State which conclusion survives an
  unresolved extension (1, 4, 8), and distinguish exact results, approximations
  and practical constraints (5, 6). Neither concealment nor an inventory of
  every possible future calculation is useful exposition.
- **Evidence language must match evidence type.** A deterministic comparison,
  a theorem, a calibrated measurement and a conjecture have different
  obligations (5–9). Shared physical reasoning does not erase those differences.

The practical outcome is a better argument, not compulsory imitation of a
particular author, length, layout or journal. Use the manuscript guide's
[natural-prose revision](physics-manuscript-writing.md#write-natural-author-led-physics-prose)
to turn the logical lessons into connected exposition, while retaining AI
provenance and human review. Use its
[substance assessment](physics-manuscript-writing.md#distinguish-a-readable-draft-from-a-submission-ready-argument)
when a draft is readable but its contribution remains unclear. This study is
not a publication-readiness certificate and does not authorize new science.
