# Comparative study of theoretical-physics writing

Historical 16-paper record. The [expanded study](physics-writing-expanded-study.md)
now indexes the 64-paper collection; the reading depths below remain unchanged.

This is an editorial companion to [the manuscript guide](physics-manuscript-writing.md),
expanded on 14 September 2026. It asks how papers make their reasoning legible,
not whether their scientific claims remain correct or whether a new project
is novel. The observations below are interpretations of inspected passages,
written in original prose; they are not quotations or author endorsements.

## Coverage and limits

The sixteen examples span conceptual proposals, short analytic results,
numerical model studies, methods and extended frameworks. They include work
by Maldacena, Witten, Polchinski, Son, Liu and other influential theorists,
but reputation is a discovery aid, not a quality criterion. The sample is
holography-heavy, with critical dynamics and analytic reconstruction included
for contrast. It is not representative of every theoretical-physics field.
Top-down examples teach exposition here; they do not change HoloForge's
bottom-up scientific identity.

The [cross-field companion](physics-writing-cross-field-study.md) adds nine
non-holographic examples, bringing the combined study to twenty-five papers.
It contrasts common reasoning habits with genre-specific evidence obligations;
the original sixteen entries below retain their recorded reading scope.

Entries 1–4 retain the first selected-section study. The expansion adds twelve
papers: five read through the main text (5, 8, 9, 11, 12) and seven inspected
in the specified sections. “Main text” includes the abstract and conclusion,
not necessarily technical appendices or every cited reference. Selected-section
reading is useful but cannot establish the quality of an entire derivation.
Versions below identify what was actually read, not necessarily the latest
revision or the final journal text. Future source-dependent scientific work
must check the relevant publication and correction record separately.

## Reading record and editorial observations

### 1. A numerical model with a physical observable

Hartnoll, Herzog and Horowitz, *Building an AdS/CFT superconductor*:
[0803.3295v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0803.3295v1).
Read: abstract and sections 1–4.

The model is introduced to answer a physical question; boundary quantities
and plotted behavior carry the result. The approximation's low-temperature
limitation appears where it affects interpretation. Lesson: introduce the
observable before asking readers to care about the computation. A compact
letter is not a template for concealing a longer paper's necessary methods.

### 2. Separate a result from its broader conjecture

Kovtun, Son and Starinets, *Viscosity in Strongly Interacting Quantum Field
Theories from Black Hole Physics*:
[hep-th/0405231v2](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0405231v2).
Read: abstract, introduction, absorption argument and concluding discussion.

A short calculation supports a definite result, followed by a broader
interpretation. That separation is worth adopting; a historical conjecture
must not be imported as an unrestricted theorem. The paper shows how a short
argument can be substantive without enumerating every computational step.

### 3. A long derivation can serve a clear physical goal

Bhattacharyya, Hubeny, Minwalla and Rangamani, *Nonlinear Fluid Dynamics from
Gravity*: [0712.2456v4](https://arxiv.org/html/0712.2456v4).
Read: abstract, introduction, section 5.5, opening of section 6 and discussion.

The systematic construction is tied to a stress tensor and its physical
consequences. Lesson: provide an organizing result so the reader knows what
the machinery produces. Do not infer from short letters that every good
physics paper should minimize derivations or avoid technical sections.

### 4. A method must recover physical information

Basar, Dunne and Yin, *Uniformizing Lee-Yang singularities*:
[published PRD 105, 105002](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.105.105002);
[arXiv record 2112.14269v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.14269v1).
Read: published abstract, introduction, model setup and conclusion.

The method is organized around the information it recovers, with examples
and comparisons making that recovery intelligible. Lesson: state the inverse
problem and what the input can constrain. Numerical agreement and reliable
singularity inference are different questions and need different evidence.

### 5. Identification, calculation, independent check

Polchinski, *Dirichlet-Branes and Ramond-Ramond Charges*:
[hep-th/9510017v3](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/9510017v3).
Read: main text.

The proposed physical identification is supported by a charge calculation
and a normalization check before broader implications are drawn. Lesson:
explain why the selected check discriminates among interpretations. Borrow
that logical economy, not the author's distinctive rhetoric or historical
extrapolations.

### 6. Explain the limit before the general proposal

Maldacena, *The Large N Limit of Superconformal Field Theories and
Supergravity*: [hep-th/9711200v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9711200v3).
Read: abstract, sections 1–2 and section 6 discussion.

The physical meaning of the limiting procedure prepares the reader for a
concrete example and a general proposal. Lesson: explain which degrees of
freedom survive and why the limit answers the question. A conceptual proposal
needs explicit assumptions and checks, not the language of completed proof.

### 7. Define the object that makes the dictionary precise

Witten, *Anti De Sitter Space And Holography*:
[hep-th/9802150v2](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/9802150v2).
Read: introduction, opening of “Ansatz For The Effective Action,” and
“Hamiltonian Interpretation.”

The exposition turns a correspondence into a question about specified
boundary data and physical quantities. Lesson: introduce definitions because
the argument needs them; explain what they allow one to calculate. This is
a useful contrast to beginning with a long catalogue of notation.

### 8. Test a prescription before extending its use

Gubser, Klebanov and Polyakov, *Gauge Theory Correlators from Non-Critical
String Theory*: [hep-th/9802109v2](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/9802109v2).
Read: main text through conclusions.

The prescription is illustrated with tractable correlators before more
ambitious strong-coupling implications. Lesson: make the role of a known-case
comparison explicit, then explain what changes in the extension. A check of
one protected quantity is not evidence for every later application.

### 9. Proposal, tractable check, qualified extension

Ryu and Takayanagi, *Holographic Derivation of Entanglement Entropy from
AdS/CFT*: [hep-th/0603001v2](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0603001v2).
Read: abstract and sections I–V, including figure captions.

A geometric proposal becomes intelligible through a low-dimensional example,
then higher-dimensional and thermal applications. Figures participate in the
reasoning. Lesson: choose an example that reveals the mechanism, not merely
an attractive plot. This style reading does not validate every historical
formula or subsequent generalization.

### 10. Distinguish the mathematical statement from physical applicability

Maldacena, Shenker and Stanford, *A bound on chaos*:
[1503.01409v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1503.01409v1).
Read: abstract and sections 2–5, including the mathematical result, physical
assumptions, examples and discussion; not the technical appendix.

The bound's mathematical ingredients and physical conditions are separated,
and examples clarify where an argument applies. Lesson: a limitation can
explain the mechanism rather than act as a disclaimer. Explicit assumptions
and occasional lists are legitimate theoretical-physics exposition.

### 11. A conceptual essay is a different genre

Van Raamsdonk, *Building up spacetime with quantum entanglement*:
[1005.3035v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1005.3035v2).
Read: main text and figure captions in this version, including the
connectivity and disentangling thought experiments.

A controlled thought experiment links qualitative pictures to quantitative
constraints. Lesson: let a picture expose a physical inference. The essay's
brevity and conversational transitions are not a substitute for the methods
and uncertainty account required by a numerical research article.

### 12. A physical disagreement can organize the derivation

Son and Stephanov, *Dynamic universality class of the QCD critical point*:
[hep-ph/0401052v2](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/0401052v2).
Read: abstract and sections I–V; not the appendix.

The observable motivation and disagreement about dynamics establish why the
mode analysis matters. A simplified sector is understood before additional
couplings are restored. Lesson: explain what an eigenmode means for physical
timescales, and compare prior claims precisely rather than dismissively.

### 13. A computational prescription needs a reason and an interpretation

Son and Starinets, *Minkowski-space correlators in AdS/CFT correspondence:
recipe and applications*:
[hep-th/0205051v2](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0205051v2).
Read: abstract, introduction, sections 3.2–3.3, section 4.3 and conclusions.

The practical gap motivates a prescription, a known case checks it, and the
relation between poles and bulk modes supplies physical meaning. Lesson:
write a reproducible method as part of an argument, not as a log of the order
in which software was developed.

### 14. Layer the navigation of an extensive framework

Crossley, Glorioso and Liu, *Effective field theory of dissipative fluids*:
[1511.03646v3](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.03646v3).
Read: abstract, I.1, opening of I.2, I.9, opening of II, setup of IV and opening
of IV.1, and VI discussion. This is selective reading of a long paper.

Motivation, organizing variables, a worked sector and broader structure give
readers several entry points. Lesson: explain why extra machinery is needed
and provide a tractable example. A long framework benefits from navigation;
it should not be forced into the architecture of a short result letter.

### 15. Compare descriptions in their shared physical regime

Hartnoll, Kovtun, Müller and Sachdev, *Theory of the Nernst effect near
quantum phase transitions in condensed matter, and in dyonic black holes*:
[0706.3215v3](https://arxiv.org/html/0706.3215v3).
Read: abstract, introduction opening through I.1 and opening of I.2,
figure 1–2 captions, and VI conclusions.

The observable motivates the model, and independent descriptions are related
through physical assumptions and a shared regime. Lesson: explain what a
baseline comparison isolates. Matching curves without matching conventions,
conditions and observables would not reproduce this argumentative strength.

### 16. An organizing formula should explain a numerical pattern

Faulkner, Liu, McGreevy and Vegh, *Emergent quantum criticality, Fermi surfaces,
and AdS2*: [0907.2694v2](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.2694v2).
Read: abstract, introduction, III.1.3, III.4, opening of IV and VII discussion.

The low-frequency expression distinguishes infrared-controlled behavior from
coefficients that retain ultraviolet information. Lesson: interpret the terms
of a central equation and show what can be learned without computing every
coefficient. This is not permission to omit numerics when they determine the
physical conclusion.

## Synthesis: choices rather than a single famous-author style

These are comparative editorial inferences, not rules asserted by the authors:

- **A physical question chooses the machinery.** Numerical studies (1),
  dynamics (12) and frameworks (14) all benefit from explaining the object of
  interest before technical detail. They differ substantially in how much
  machinery the central argument actually requires.
- **Logical order is not always the same order.** Proposal/example/extension
  (6, 9), result/assumptions/proof (10), and organizing construction/applications
  (3, 14, 16) are all useful. Choose the dependence appropriate to the result.
- **Checks need interpretive jobs.** The charge comparison (5), correlator
  examples (8, 13) and independent descriptions (15) illustrate why a check
  matters. A pass count without that explanation is research-record content.
- **Qualifications belong to the argument.** Separate calculated result from
  conjecture (2), theorem from applicability (10), and universal behavior from
  model-dependent amplitude (16). Avoid both overclaiming and repetitive
  disclaimers that obscure a genuine result.
- **Concise does not mean uniformly short.** An essay's thought experiment
  (11), a compact letter (5) and a systematic framework (14) have different
  obligations. Lists, derivations and technical appendices are tools, not
  stylistic defects by themselves.

## Apply and test the lessons

For an authorized revision, privately record: the principal physical question;
the closest two or more contrasting argument types; the adopted lesson and
its reason; where substantial text moves; and how the evidence is preserved.
This is not a new fixed reading quota. Extend the corpus when it lacks the
relevant physics community, contribution type or counterexample.

Check whether the introduction makes the question precise, every section has
a reason to follow its predecessor, central equations receive interpretation,
and the conclusion answers the opening question. Then separately verify that
values, assumptions, failed checks, uncertainties and source limitations were
not changed by the rewrite. Better exposition is not new scientific evidence,
and this study provides neither publication-readiness nor novelty clearance.
