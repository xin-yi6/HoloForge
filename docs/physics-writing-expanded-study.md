# A broader study of physics writing

This 14 September 2026 expansion asks which explanatory practices recur across
physics, and which depend on the kind of contribution. It adds **39 complete
main-text readings** to the earlier 25-paper collection: **64 distinct papers,
50 complete main-text readings and 14 selected-section readings** in total.
The [machine-readable corpus](physics-writing-corpus.json) records every paper,
primary-source link, version/read scope, and the new readings' PDF hashes.
The [original study](physics-writing-reading-study.md) and
[cross-field study](physics-writing-cross-field-study.md) remain historical
records; their selected readings have not been silently upgraded.

## What the study can establish

These are AI-assisted editorial interpretations of primary texts, in original
prose. Complete main text includes the abstract, argument and ending, but not
every appendix, supplement, cited work or an independent rederivation. The
ledger identifies exclusions. The sources' scientific accuracy, novelty,
subsequent corrections and present-day empirical status were not audited here.
Versions identify the actual reading, not necessarily the latest publication.
Historical PDFs sometimes display later compilation dates; those do not date
the original scientific contribution.

The sample includes gravity, particle theory, cosmology, quantum foundations,
quantum information, condensed matter, stochastic thermodynamics, active
matter and computational many-body physics. Earlier observational material
is a contrast case, not a newly completed full reading. Short letters,
longer derivations, mechanisms, protocols, classifications, consistency
arguments and numerical methods pose different explanatory demands.

This is a **purposive, not representative sample**. Influential English-language,
accessible papers and repeated author groups are overrepresented. Neither
fame nor a larger count proves a universal writing law. Fifty complete
readings was the target for this expansion, **not a recurring quota** for
manuscript revision. The appropriate conclusion is a set of transferable
practices with exceptions, not a universal sentence template. More reading
must not become a substitute for improving the current argument or doing
necessary science.

## How to inspect the evidence

The corpus uses H01–H16 for the original study, X01–X09 for its cross-field
extension, and E01–E39 for the new readings. Those are editorial record IDs,
not scientific support levels. Each new entry has one short observation;
the twelve closer comparisons below examine paragraph, equation and ending
choices. They are interpretations, not independent reviewer agreement or a
quantitative prevalence analysis. A paper can illustrate several practices
without every sentence being exemplary.

For reuse, select two to four examples whose **argumentative job** resembles
the draft. Read their relevant passages rather than merely copying this
checklist. Add a missing genre when needed; do not reread fifty papers for
every revision. Keep the full ledger as agent-readable context and consult
the shorter [manuscript guide](physics-manuscript-writing.md) during drafting.

## Twelve close comparisons

### 1. A criterion: Peres (E01)

[Separability Criterion for Density Matrices](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9604005v2)
first distinguishes separability from a weaker familiar restriction. The
definition and transpose calculation earn the proposed test; examples then
establish which implications hold. The ending's dimension-dependent
qualification changes the logical claim, not merely its tone. Transfer:
organize comparisons by what they exclude. Exception: a necessary criterion
must not become a sufficient one just to give the abstract a cleaner headline.

### 2. A physically motivated bound: Barato and Seifert (E03)

[Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relation for Biomolecular Processes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05944v2)
starts with the cost of biological precision. A simple walk gives the formula
physical meaning before networks introduce generality. The final distinction
between proved cases and numerical support is indispensable. Compare the
later bound/proof architecture in [Gingrich et al. (E06)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02212v3):
the role of evidence changes, so the language must change too. Neither a
successful plot nor a famous subsequent result retroactively supplies a proof.

### 3. An average: Page (E04)

[Average Entropy of a Subsystem](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9305007v2)
specifies the ensemble before evaluating its entropy. The comparison with
entropy of the averaged state prevents a plausible misreading of the central
quantity. Finite examples, a conjectured expression and an asymptotic result
are distinguishable. The ending interprets where information resides.
Transfer: introduce the averaging operation before an attractive number.
Exception: exploratory calculation history in a historical paper does not
require a new manuscript to reproduce its software-development diary.

### 4. A constructive proposal: Fu and Kane (E11)

[Superconducting Proximity Effect and Majorana Fermions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.1692v3)
uses an effective analogy while stating how the full model differs. Vortex,
line, junction and circuit are cumulative constructions: each supplies an
operation the next needs. Solvable limits make the diagrams intelligible;
implementation demands follow. Transfer: explain why another component is
being introduced. Exception: analogy does not license importing the entire
symmetry or experimental status of the comparison system.

### 5. A material prediction: Bernevig, Hughes and Zhang (E12)

[Quantum Spin Hall Effect in HgTe Quantum Wells](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0611399v1)
connects symmetry, a minimal effective model and material-specific parameter
information. A completion of the model distinguishes an absolute invariant
from its change; proposed electrical tests close the chain of reasoning.
Transfer: let simple analysis explain what the detailed calculation measures.
Exception: selecting a beautiful minimal model cannot replace the numerical
or material input on which a concrete prediction depends.

### 6. A classification: Kitaev (E13)

[Periodic Table for Topological Insulators and Superconductors](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.2686v2)
announces an organizing table, teaches its objects through examples, and then
handles equivalence, symmetries and dimension. The interaction discussion
exposes a limit of the free classification. Tables and technical vocabulary
are central to this argument. Transfer: give readers an overview before a
systematic construction. Exception: neither shortness nor deferred proof
details should be mistaken for a complete scientific verification.

### 7. A reversed implication: Jacobson (E19)

[Thermodynamics of Spacetime](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9504004v2)
prepares the reader with elementary thermodynamics before reversing the
usual direction of explanation. Heat, entropy and temperature receive
operational identifications before their relation yields an equation.
The ending asks what equilibrium means for the interpretation. Transfer:
motivate identifications instead of presenting a dictionary by assertion.
Exception: an equation-of-state argument is not a microscopic construction;
an evocative analogy must not close that gap rhetorically.

### 8. A scaling question: Srednicki (E21)

[Entropy and Area](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9303048v2)
asks what the entropy can depend on, gives an intuitive scaling argument,
and solves a small oscillator problem that supplies the field-theory method.
The plot tests the predicted dependence rather than displaying solver effort.
Other dimensions identify where the intuition changes. Transfer: use an
example to expose both mechanism and method. Exception: its historical
numerical accuracy is not a tolerance prescription for a different claim.

### 9. An existence theorem: Weinberg (E27)

[Adiabatic Modes in Cosmology](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0302326v1)
motivates conservation through the need to connect cosmological eras, states
the extension assumption, and distinguishes existence of an adiabatic mode
from properties of arbitrary modes. A model and a gauge comparison explain
why a seemingly contradictory conclusion can arise. Transfer: make the
quantifier and order of limits visible. Exception: this longer proof needs
navigation that a four-page geometric argument may not need.

### 10. An operational proposal: Georgi (E32)

[Unparticle Physics](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0703260v3)
turns a question about what an unfamiliar sector would look like into
effective-theory matching and an illustrative spectrum. A familiar limit
orients the result; the ending separates an example from a broader program.
Transfer: ask what the formal object would change in a measurable quantity.
Exception: the distinctive conversational voice is not a template to imitate,
and a deliberately simple example is not an optimized phenomenological study.

### 11. A missing mechanism: Goldberger and Wise (E33)

[Modulus Stabilization with Bulk Fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9907447v2)
addresses a specific missing ingredient in an existing scenario. A bulk field
and boundary terms produce a potential; its minimum answers that deficiency.
Backreaction and finite-coupling estimates address concrete objections.
The ending retains the separate fine-tuning problem. Transfer: explain each
robustness check by the physical objection it answers. Exception: necessary
approximations belong in the argument, not in an inaccessible audit log.

### 12. Computational access: Carleo and Troyer (E39)

[Solving the Quantum Many-Body Problem with Artificial Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02318v1)
connects a representational bottleneck to a variational state and physical
ground-state and dynamical observables. Comparisons explain what the new
representation buys in the tested systems. The transition to dynamics is
motivated by a different physical task. Transfer: computational access can
be a substantive contribution. Exception: finite model demonstrations do
not establish unrestricted representability, optimization success or removal
of every sign/phase problem; enthusiastic historical wording is not authority.

## Recurring practices, with counterexamples

| Editorial inference | Evidence in this corpus | When to adapt it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explain the physical obstacle that makes machinery necessary. | E02 entropy definition; E19 spacetime thermodynamics; E33 stabilization; E39 many-body representation | A short theorem paper may state its result first, provided its question and assumptions remain clear. |
| Make an equation change what the reader can infer. | E04 average versus averaged state; E27 mode existence; E32 spectrum | A long framework needs definitions and intermediate identities; not every equation needs a separate interpretation paragraph. |
| Separate claim, evidence and extension. | E03 bound; E12 material prediction; E24 charges; E39 computation | Direct claims need not carry a repeated disclaimer; retain the qualification where it changes the inference. |
| Choose a comparison that isolates a physical distinction. | E01 logical criterion; E09 invariant; E21 dimensional scaling; E36 localization constraints | A methods comparison may chiefly establish access or efficiency rather than a new phenomenon. |
| Let the conclusion answer the opening question. | E07 excess heat; E19 equation of state; E33 stabilized modulus | A proposal may end with an unresolved construction, but cannot describe that construction as completed. |
| Use structure according to argumentative work. | E13 table; E18 numbered protocol; E27 long proof; E32 conversational proposal | Lists, headings, first-person prose, passive voice and different paragraph lengths are not inherently unphysical or AI-like. |

These are cross-field observations, not prevalence estimates or proof of
universality. The mechanism, quantifiers and evidence obligations matter more
than identical introduction lengths, sentence rhythm, or a famous surname.
Examples with different outcomes are essential: an exact theorem, useful
approximation, numerical indication and open conjecture should not all read
as the same category of achievement.

## Applying the study to a draft

Make three passes, preserving the original and the scientific record:

1. **Argument:** state the question and principal result; choose an appropriate
   architecture; explain how each section earns the next. Compare with two or
   more matched examples and one contrasting architecture.
2. **Exposition:** give paragraphs distinct jobs, explain consequential
   equations, make comparisons informative, and remove generic connective
   padding. Keep necessary numerical detail and scientific qualifications.
3. **Preservation:** map revised claims, numbers, uncertainty meanings,
   assumptions and omitted material to the evidence. A fluent sentence cannot
   resolve a failed test or turn a model result into an empirical finding.

Keep a short private change map explaining which lessons fit and which were
rejected. Assess the resulting manuscript by reading it, not by word counts
or the number of cited style examples. Automated corpus checks establish
record consistency and navigation, **not writing quality**. Preserve AI-use
provenance and author review; neither a natural voice nor this study establishes
human authorship, publication readiness or scientific novelty.
